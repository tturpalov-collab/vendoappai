87 % of August declines are issuer decisions 110 of 126 declines came from the card issuer, most often insufficient funds — outside anyone's control. Technical failures are down to 16 across 12,171 attempts, or 0.13 %. There is essentially nothing left to fix on the infrastructure side.

The 338 “no host response” events in June came from a platform-side incident rather than anything at the sites: terminals were not receiving a reply from the payment host, with the peak in the week of 22 June. By July this was down to 20 cases, and by August to two.

5One terminal needs attention

221400014646 — Driven The second-highest earning terminal of the quarter at 38,441 AED. Its last sale was on 13 August — thirteen days of silence, and it no longer appears in the August top five. At its earlier rate of roughly 17,400 AED per month, the downtime has cost about 7,500 AED so far.

Two other terminals stopped in the same period without cause for concern. 221400016990 (Innovo Ghaff Woods, marked OLD) stopped on 11 August, and 221400013955 (Innovo Ghaf Woods) began trading the same day — a unit replacement at the same site rather than a loss.

6Demand profile

More than half of all revenue falls into the four hours around lunch, and weekends are close to empty.

Revenue by hour of day, AED. The peak at 12:00 alone carries 18 % of revenue; the 11:00–14:00 window carries 54.4 %.

Revenue by day of week, AED. Saturday and Sunday together account for 1.2 %; Friday already runs 36 % below a typical weekday.

This is an office-hours profile in its purest form, consistent with the sites themselves. The practical implication is scheduling: restocking and servicing before 08:00 or after 18:00 costs almost nothing in lost sales, while the same downtime at lunchtime is several times more expensive.

The five strongest days of the quarter all fell in the second half of August — the 24th (13,419 AED), 25th, 19th, 18th and 20th.

7Payment mix and service quality

Payments are entirely cashless. Not a single cash payment was recorded in any of the three months. Visa accounts for 72 % of transactions and Mastercard for 28 %.

Ticket sizes cluster tightly. Payments between 10 and 50 AED make up 68 % of transactions and 87 % of revenue.

Service speed held steady as the network grew: 3.7–3.9 seconds per transaction and 7.3–7.5 seconds to dispense, with no deterioration between June and August. Cancelled dispenses stayed between 0.16 % and 0.26 %.

Product-level reporting is not yet available: the product identifiers arriving from the machines are not human-readable, so what sells and what does not cannot be broken out. This is being addressed on the integration side.
